Section 1: Concept of Proportional Division
Section Identity: Concept of Proportional Division
Core Understanding: Proportional division is the process of partitioning a total quantity (such as money, land, or weight) into unequal parts based on a specified ratio (e.g., 3:5), where the total is split into equal unit parts distributed according to each party's ratio term.
Visual-First Explanation: A single rectangular bar representing 320 LE breaks into 8 equal unit blocks (3+5=8). Person A receives 3 unit blocks and Person B receives 5 unit blocks. Each individual unit block represents a constant value of 40 LE (320÷8).
Visual Scene Script:
A total bar of 320 LE appears at the top.
The bar divides into 8 equal segment blocks representing the sum of ratio parts (3+5=8).
3 blocks glow blue for Person A, and 5 blocks glow orange for Person B.
Each block displays a unit value of 40 LE (320÷8=40), yielding shares of 120 LE and 200 LE.
Dynamic View Blueprint: Interactive stack of unit blocks with draggable ratio dividers, sum-of-parts calculator, and real-time total sum verification readout.
Visual Learning Outcome: Student understands that proportional division divides a total based on unit share allocation rather than equal splitting.
Concept Check: If a total quantity is divided in the ratio 2:3, how many equal unit parts is the whole quantity divided into?

Section 4: Comparison & Practical Applications of Central Tendency
Section Identity: Comparison & Practical Applications of Central Tendency
Core Understanding: Each measure of central tendency serves a distinct analytical purpose:
Arithmetic Mean is optimal for symmetric data without extreme values (outliers).
Median is the most robust measure when datasets contain extreme outliers, as it remains unaffected by extreme values.
Mode is preferred for categorical data or identifying popular preferences.
Visual-First Explanation: A balance scale holds data points 2,3,4,5,6 with Mean = 4 and Median = 4. Adding an extreme outlier value 100 causes the mean pointer to shoot right to 20 (skewed away from the data cluster), while the median pointer shifts only slightly to 4.5, remaining securely anchored inside the dense data group.
